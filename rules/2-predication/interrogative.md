## I. Core Root

The root **`wei`** carries the core meaning of "question / unknown / what." It is 1-syllable and toneless (see `0-foundation/tone-prosody.md` §II-B). On its own, `wei` can be used as a general expression of uncertainty ("huh?" / "what?").

When combined with a colour prefix, the resulting 2-syllable question word remains toneless — consistent with the 1–2 syllable toneless rule.

## II. Wh-Questions — Colour Prefix + `wei`

| Colour | Prefix | Question Word | Philosophy Link |
|:---|:---|:---|:---|
| **Brown** | `ae-` | **Who?** / **Whose?** | 自我 / 他者 — personhood and identity |
| **Red** | `a-` | **Which?** | 犧牲 / 覺悟 — choosing between options |
| **Yellow** | `e-` | **What?** | 課題 / 意義 — the thing sought or defined |
| **Blue** | `i-` | **Where?** | 孤離 / 並存 — space and location |
| **White** | `o-` | **When?** | 遺憾 / 可能性 — time and its passage |
| **Green** | `u-` | **How?** | 救贖 / 信念 — method, path, process |
| **Black** | `y-` | **Why?** | 敬畏 / 探求 — reason and deeper cause |

### A. Tone

Prefixed question words are **toneless** (flat mid-tone). The colour prefix is an external proclitic (see `1-nominals/nouns-colour-prefix.md` §III). All 1–2 syllable words in Kilor are toneless (see `0-foundation/tone-prosody.md` §II-B).

| Construction | Spelling |
|:---|:---|
| `a + wei` | `awei` |
| `e + wei` | `ewei` |
| `i + wei` | `iwei` |
| `o + wei` | `owei` |
| `u + wei` | `uwei` |
| `y + wei` | `ywei` |
| `ae + wei` | `aewei` |
| `ae + weisan` | `aeweisan` |

### B. Colour Prefix Rules

The colour prefix follows standard rules from `1-nominals/nouns-colour-prefix.md`: external proclitic, flat mid-tone, does not trigger Contrastive Suffix Rule. Question words are **formally exempt** from the definiteness rule — their colour prefixes encode question type, not definiteness.

> **Hyphenation:** Question words use the merged form without a hyphen (`awei`, `ewei`, `iwei`, `owei`, `uwei`, `ywei`, `aewei`). This is an explicit exception to the mandatory hyphen rule in `1-nominals/nouns-colour-prefix.md` §II-A.

### C. Case Marking Exemption

Question words are **exempt from case marking** — even when fronted in non-SOV word order (see §III), they carry no Accusative or Genitive suffix. The fronting rule alone identifies them as the interrogative element.

This exemption overrides the general rule requiring case suffixes in non-SOV order (see `0-foundation/grammar-syntax.md` §I-B). Question words are always fronted, so they would *always* trigger the mandatory-case rule — the exemption avoids this conflict.

#### Animate-Ambiguity Rule

When a fronted question word creates animate-subject vs. animate-object ambiguity, the **non-interrogative argument must carry its appropriate case suffix**. The question word itself remains exempt:

> `aewei si taka?` — "Who eats him/her?" (`si` unmarked = subject)
> `aewei sin taka?` — "He/she eats whom?" (`sin` carries ACC)

### D. Genitive Question — `aeweisan` (Whose?)

Fused form: `ae-` (proclitic) + `weisan` (2-syllable root, toneless).

| Form | Meaning |
|:---|:---|
| `aewei` | Who? (subject or object) |
| `aeweisan` | Whose? (genitive) |

> `aeweisan fora taka?` — "Whose fire burns?"

## III. Word Order — Fronting

All question words are **fronted** to the beginning of the sentence, regardless of grammatical role. This is strict — question words cannot remain in situ.

> `awei fora taka?` — "Which (one) eats fire?"
> `ewei ti taka?` — "What do you eat?"
> `iwei fora?` — "Where is fire?"
> `ywei ti fora taka?` — "Why do you eat fire?"

## IV. Yes/No Questions — `iu` Particle

### A. Polar Questions

Append `iu` to the end of a declarative sentence. Intonation rises on `iu`.

> **Statement:** `ti fora taka.` (You eat fire.)
> **Question:** `ti fora taka iu?` (Do you eat fire?)

Answered using standalone particles from `2-predication/negation.md`:
> **A:** `iu.` (Yes.) / `na.` (No.)

### B. Rhetorical Questions — 反問句

Formed by combining `nar` with `iu`:

> `ti fora taka nar iu?` — "Don't you eat fire?" (implies: *you do eat fire*)
> Contrast: `ti fora taka iu?` — neutral question, no presupposition
