## I. Core Philosophy

Kilor expresses conditionals through two closed-class particles: `li` ("if") and `bam` ("then," consequential). These follow the same adverbial subordination pattern as other clause-level particles (`tu`, `aiga`, `hoskar` — see `2-predication/subordination.md` §IV). Conditionals use a single form regardless of realis/irrealis status — context disambiguates.

All conditional particles are **toneless, closed-class, 1-syllable function words** exempt from the `-s` constraint (see `0-foundation/grammar-syntax.md` §IV-C).

---

## II. Particles

| Particle | Meaning | Type |
|:---|:---|:---|
| `li` | if | Conditional subordinator |
| `bam` | then / so / consequently | Consequential particle (便/就) |

`bam` serves double duty — as the correlative "then" in conditional constructions, and as a general consequential connector between clauses ("X happened, bam Y happened").

---

## III. Conditional Constructions

### A. Pattern 1 — Correlative (if…then)

The full correlative pattern: `li`-clause first, then `bam`-clause.

**Structure:** `li [condition], bam [result]`

| Kilor | Meaning |
|:---|:---|
| `li ti kau, bam ki losto res` | "If you come, then I am happy" |
| `li hupli hup, bam ki kau nar` | "If rain falls, then I do not come" |

### B. Pattern 2 — Postposed Condition (no `bam`)

The `li`-clause may follow the main clause, with no `bam`:

**Structure:** `[result] li [condition]`

| Kilor | Meaning |
|:---|:---|
| `ki losto res li ti kau` | "I am happy if you come" |
| `ki kau nar li hupli hup` | "I do not come if rain falls" |

When the condition is postposed, `bam` is not used. Using `bam` in this position would be ungrammatical.

---

## IV. `bam` as General Consequential (便/就)

`bam` also functions as a standalone consequential connector between clauses — not requiring `li`. It marks that the second clause follows naturally from the first:

| Kilor | Meaning |
|:---|:---|
| `si rilda, bam ki kau` | "He spoke, then I came" |
| `ti bau taka, bam thep` | "You eat bread, then (you) sleep" |

This usage parallels the Chinese 便/就: the second action is a direct consequence of the first. It is not a temporal sequence marker (for that, use bare juxtaposition or `ei` — see `2-predication/clause-combining.md`).

## V. Interaction with Other Systems

### A. Negation

`nar` scopes over the clause it appears in. In conditional constructions, either clause or both may be negated:

| Kilor | Meaning |
|:---|:---|
| `li ti kau nar, bam ki hik res` | "If you do not come, then I am sad" |
| `li ti kau, bam ki hik res nar` | "If you come, then I am not sad" |

### B. Emotional Particles

Emotional particles (see `3-subsystems/colour-emotion.md`) may appear on either clause independently:

| Kilor | Meaning |
|:---|:---|
| `e, li ti kau, bam ki losto res` | "(With joy,) if you come, then I am happy" |
| `y, li ti kau, bam ki losto res` | "(With fear,) if you come, then I am happy" |

### C. Aspect

Aspect particles (`gin`, `ger`, `gou` — see `3-subsystems/aspect.md`) operate within each clause independently:

> `li ti kau ger, bam ki taka ger` — "If you have come, then I have eaten"
