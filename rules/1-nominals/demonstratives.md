## I. Overview

Kilor has a **2-way distance contrast** in demonstratives, reusing the existing spatial roots `thin` ("here") and `tha` ("there") as dual-citizenship words: they remain Category G place nouns for locative use, but also function as **demonstrative determiners** and **demonstrative pronouns**.

---

## II. Distance Contrast

| Demonstrative | Root | Literal Meaning | Proximity |
|---|---|---|---|
| `thin` | here | "this" | Speaker-proximate |
| `tha` | there | "that" | Distal |

No far-distal ("yonder") form exists. `tha` covers all non-proximate referents.

---

## III. Syntactic Uses

### A. Demonstrative Determiner

`thin` / `tha` precede the noun phrase. The demonstrative **replaces the colour prefix** — the noun appears as a bare root, since the demonstrative inherently marks definiteness and deixis overrides ontological classification:

> `thin bau` — "this bread"
> `tha maeha` — "that person"
> `thin slato` — "this house"

#### Emotional Override (異體字)

When the speaker wishes to express an emotional stance toward the referent, the colour prefix is **retained** after the demonstrative. This follows the standard 異體字 pattern (see `1-nominals/nouns-colour-prefix.md` §VI):

> `thin a-bau` — "this bread" (Red emotional colouring: anger towards the bread)
> `tha o-slato` — "that house" (White emotional colouring: surprise or regret about the house)
> `thin u-lunla` — "this tree" (Green emotional colouring: calm/faith regarding the tree)

Without emotional override, the bare root is the default:

> `thin bau` — "this bread" (neutral)
> `thin a-bau` — "this bread" (emotional: Red/anger)

### B. Demonstrative Pronoun

`thin` / `tha` can stand alone as bare pronominal arguments (subject or object):

> `thin gor res.` — "This is good."
> `tha bono res.` — "That is bad."
> `ki mug thin.` — "I want this."
> `ti sopa tha iu?` — "Do you see that?"

### C. Propositional Anaphora (Discourse Deixis)

`thin` / `tha` may refer to an entire preceding clause, proposition, or situation — equivalent to English "this" / "that" referring to a fact the speaker has just stated or heard:

> `ki asdo tha.` — "I know that." (tha refers to a previously stated fact)
> `thin gor res.` — "This is good." (thin refers to a situation/clause just mentioned)
> `tha hap res.` — "That is right." (tha refers to what was just said)

When `thin`/`tha` serves as a propositional complement of a cognition or communication verb, no `kus` subordinator is needed — the demonstrative alone is the complement, following the existing optional-`kus` pattern for non-clausal complements (see `2-predication/subordination.md` §II-B):

> `ki asdo tha.` — "I know that." (not `ki asdo kus tha`)

This is distinct from `kus`-complement clauses (`ki asdo kus ti kau.` — "I know that you came."), where `kus` embeds a full clause rather than an anaphoric pronoun.

## IV. Position in the NP

Demonstratives occupy the **leftmost slot** of the noun phrase, before possessors and adjectives. See `0-foundation/grammar-syntax.md` §I-E for the full NP-internal word order.

```
[Demo] — [Poss GEN] — [Adj] — [(Colour-)Noun] — [Num/Quant] — [Rel Clause]
```

> `thin kis ralis bau ro kus ki taka ger`
> "these three big breads of mine that I ate"
>
> thin — kis — ralis — bau — ro — [kus ki taka ger]
> this — my — big — bread — three — that I ate

### Interaction with Case

When a demonstrative-pronoun receives case, the case suffix attaches to the demonstrative:

> `ki mug thinna.` — "I want this." (ACC, last vowel `i` = front → back suffix `-na`)
> `thinsa wino res gor.` — "This thing's colour is good." (GEN, last vowel `i` = front → back suffix `-sa`)

## V. Interaction with the Colour Prefix System

| Construction | Form | Definiteness | Emotional |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bare noun | `bau` | Indefinite | Neutral |
| Colour-prefixed noun | `e-bau` | Definite | Neutral (Yellow = crafted class) |
| Demonstrative + bare noun | `thin bau` | Definite (deictic) | Neutral |
| Demonstrative + colour-prefixed noun | `thin e-bau` | Definite (deictic) | Emotional (異體字) |
