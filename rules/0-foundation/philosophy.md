## I. The Chromatic-Philosophical Mapping

Kilor's culture is built upon **7 fundamental dual-concepts**, each mapped to one of the 7 colour prefixes. These are not merely abstract philosophy — they inform the semantic intuition behind each colour's ontological classification of nouns.

---

## II. The Seven Dual-Concepts

### Blue — `i-` — 孤離 (Solitude) / 並存 (Coexistence)

> **The most central idea in Kilor culture.**

Every being in the world is unique — no two creatures are identical. This uniqueness means we must ultimately face a state of **solitude (孤離)**. No other being can fully share our internal experience.

Yet this same difference among beings demonstrates that **all are unique and co-exist in this world (並存)**. Solitude and coexistence are two sides of the same coin — each proves the other.

**Ontological Implication (Blue prefix):** Fluids, gases, vast boundary-less spaces — things that are simultaneously connected (coexisting in a shared medium) and fundamentally unbound (each particle or current following its own path).

---

### Red — `a-` — 犧牲 (Sacrifice) / 覺悟 (Awakening)

Everything has a price. To gain something, you must **lose something (犧牲)**. There is no creation without destruction, no growth without cost.

But these sacrifices define who you are. Only those who **recognise and bear the cost of their choices can go further (覺悟)** — this awakening is the redemptive counterpart to sacrifice.

**Ontological Implication (Red prefix):** Living, breathing, sentient beings — entities that consume energy (sacrifice) and act with awareness (awakening).

---

### Brown — `ae-` — 自我 (Ego) / 他者 (Other)

We each have our own **ego, our internal self (自我)**, with its desires, boundaries, and identity. But the world and other beings run according to their own ways, as **others (他者)**.

The **friction between self and other** — between what we want and what the world does — is the root of much pain in life. Brown represents this boundary and the friction it creates.

**Ontological Implication (Brown prefix):** Earth, wood, mundane physical boundaries — the literal and metaphorical ground where self meets world, where friction occurs.

---

### Black — `y-` — 敬畏 (Reverence/Awe) / 探求 (Exploration)

Things may not go as you will. There are forces — nature, fate, the unknown — beyond your control. One must hold **reverence and awe (敬畏)** toward this reality, being prepared for what lies outside your mindset.

But this does not mean giving up on a changing world. We **adapt and learn to live with and explore the dynamic world (探求)**. We probe the unknown even as we respect its power.

**Ontological Implication (Black prefix):** Hard, dense, unyielding matter — the deep rock, the heavy mass that resists our will (reverence) but rewards those who dig deeper (exploration).

---

### White — `o-` — 遺憾 (Regret) / 可能性 (Possibility)

Through the passage of time, whether one acts or does not act, there is always only one outcome at a time — and that outcome may not be what you wanted. This gap between what is and what could have been creates **regret (遺憾)**.

But through that same passage of time, the **ethereal uncertainty of possibility (可能性)** has the chance to become real. What is formless now may take shape. Regret and possibility are bound by time.

**Ontological Implication (White prefix):** Abstract, formless, purely conceptual things — the void from which possibility emerges, and into which regret echoes.

---

### Yellow — `e-` — 課題 (Quest) / 意義 (Meaning)

Life has no inherent meaning. But humans need meaning to survive — and this very need creates pain, an existential hunger that cannot be permanently satisfied. The search for **meaning (意義)** is a fundamental human condition.

Yet this does not stop us from **defining our own quest, our own life's work (課題)**. We choose — we craft — we build purpose through action. The quest is the answer to meaning's absence.

**Ontological Implication (Yellow prefix):** Human-crafted tools, designed objects — the physical manifestation of quest and meaning-making, things built with intention.

---

### Green — `u-` — 救贖 (Salvation) / 信念 (Faith)

The difference between ego and the world causes our pain. Only by **adapting and recognising that both self and other can co-exist** can we find **salvation (救贖)**. This is the path of integration — accepting the world as it is while remaining true to oneself.

On the other hand, knowing that things may not go as we will, but still **holding your faith and following it (信念)**, is another path. This is the path of conviction — standing firm even when the outcome is uncertain.

**Ontological Implication (Green prefix):** Organic growth, networks, ecosystems — things that thrive through adaptation (salvation through integration) and persist through deep-rooted growth (faith as enduring life).

## IV. Philosophical Influence on the Language

These dual-concepts are not merely decorative. They inform the design of Kilor at every level:

- **The Chromatic Ontology System** (colour prefixes) is the direct linguistic embodiment — each prefix's semantic field resonates with its philosophical dual-concept
- **The 4-category tonal system** reflects the rhythm of these tensions: Noun (grounded descent), Verb (dynamic rise), Adjective (arched prominence), Adverb (dipped grounding)[^1]
- **The tolerance for ambiguity** (fault-tolerant prefixes, identical contour abutment, optional case marking) echoes the cultural embrace of duality — things need not be forced into a single category

[^1]: The "arched prominence" (M→H→H) and "dipped grounding" (M→L→M) metaphors describe the 3+ syllable tone patterns. For 1–2 syllable words, Adjective and Adverb are derived from Noun and Verb via `-s` and share their parent contours: Adjective = Noun's H→L ("grounded descent"), Adverb = Verb's L→H ("dynamic rise"). See `0-foundation/tone-prosody.md` §II-B, §II-C.
