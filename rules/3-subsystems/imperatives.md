Kilor expresses commands through a **bare-verb base** plus optional **register particles**. There is no morphological imperative — no conjugation, no tone change. All imperative particles are closed-class, toneless function words (see `0-foundation/grammar-syntax.md` §IV-C).

## I. Imperative Register System

| Register | Particle | Position | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Casual command** | (bare verb) | — | `Taka!` — "Eat!" |
| **Casual negative** | `nar` | Post-verbal | `Taka nar!` — "Don't eat!" |
| **Suggestion** | `sor` | Sentence-final | `Taka sor.` — "Let's eat." (≈ Cantonese 啦) |
| **Polite request** | `chom` | Pre-verbal | `Chom taka.` — "Please eat." (≈ 請) |
| **Strong prohibition** | `maug` | Pre-verbal | `Maug taka!` — "Do not eat!" (≈ 咪) |

### A. Casual Command — Bare Verb

> `Kau!` — "Come!"
> `Taka!` — "Eat!"

Subject (second person) implied and omitted.

### B. Negative Command — Verb + `nar`

> `Taka nar!` — "Don't eat!"

Consistent with Kilor's standard postpositive negation rule (`2-predication/negation.md` §II-A).

### C. Suggestion — `sor` (Sentence-Final)

> `Taka sor.` — "Let's eat."
> `Kau sor.` — "Come, won't you?"
> `Taka nar sor.` — "(I suggest) don't eat."

`sor` ≈ Cantonese 啦 (laa1) / Mandarin 吧 (ba).

### D. Polite Request — `chom` (Pre-Verbal)

> `Chom taka.` — "Please eat."
> `Chom minau.` — "Please stand/stay."

`chom` ≈ Cantonese 請 (cing2).

### E. Strong Prohibition — `maug` (Pre-Verbal)

> `Maug taka!` — "Do not eat!" (strong)

Distinction from `nar`:
- `Taka nar!` = neutral "don't eat"
- `Maug taka!` = prohibitive with speaker authority (≈ Cantonese 咪 mai5)

## II. Imperative with Emotional Particles

Emotional particles may co-occur (see `3-subsystems/colour-emotion.md`):

> `i Taka nar!` — "Don't eat!" (with sadness)
> `o Chom kau.` — "Please come." (with reverence)

## III. Vocative — `hei` ("hello / hey")

`hei` is a vocative interjection:

> `Hei!` — "Hello!" / "Hey!"
> `Hei, song!` — "Hey, friend!"

### Colour Prefix on Vocative

A colour prefix may optionally attach to `hei` for emotional nuance (異體字 principle — see `1-nominals/nouns-colour-prefix.md` §VI):

| Form | Emotional Nuance |
|---|---|
| `a-hei` | Calling with passion or anger |
| `e'hei` | Calling with warmth, cheerful |
| `i'hei` | Calling with melancholy |
| `o'hei` | Calling with reverence, formal |
| `u'hei` | Calling with joy |
| `y'hei` | Calling with curiosity or surprise |
| `ae'hei` | Calling dismissively, condescending |

> `ae'hei, ti!` — "Hey, you!" (dismissive)

### Vocative Position

Clause-initially or standalone; doesn't affect the colour prefix system of the clause.

---

## IV. Interaction with Other Systems

### A. Interrogatives

`iu` does not combine with imperatives. Use a standard yes/no question with `chom`:
> `ti chom kau iu?` — "Will you please come?"

### B. Aspect

Aspect particles may appear (see `3-subsystems/aspect.md`):
> `Taka ger!` — "Eat (have eaten)!" — urging completion
