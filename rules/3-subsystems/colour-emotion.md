## I. Historical Origin — One Form, Two Roles

The 7 colour morphemes (`a`, `e`, `i`, `o`, `u`, `y`, `ae`) serve two distinct functions: **ontological classification** (noun prefixes) and **emotional register** (standalone particles). Both descend from the same grammaticalization path:

1. **Pre-history:** Colour terms were full content words with ontological and emotional associations.
2. **Early Kilor:** Terms eroded into noun classifiers. Emotional connotations persisted as cultural resonance.
3. **Classical Kilor:** Emotional connotations were systematized into a parallel grammatical function — standalone particles.
4. **Modern Kilor:** Particles settled into 7 basic, universally recognizable emotions. The philosophical dual-concepts in `0-foundation/philosophy.md` remain as the historical foundation; the modern system foregrounds intuitive emotional experience.

This dual function parallels the Spanish diminutive `-ito` — same form, two related functions, disambiguated by syntactic position.

For extended usage (rhetoric, artistic layering, example gallery), see `guide/emotional-register-usage.md`.

## II. The 7 Basic Emotions

| Colour | Particle | Emotion | High-Arousal (+) | Low-Arousal (−) |
|:---|:---|:---|:---|:---|
| **Red** | `a` | **Anger** | Rage, fury, outburst | Irritation, annoyance, simmering |
| **Yellow** | `e` | **Joy** | Ecstasy, excitement, elation | Contentment, mild pleasure |
| **Blue** | `i` | **Sadness** | Grief, despair, weeping | Melancholy, wistfulness, quiet ache |
| **Green** | `u` | **Calm** | Serenity, bliss, deep peace | Quiet ease, gentle relaxation |
| **Black** | `y` | **Fear** | Terror, panic, paralysis | Worry, unease, wariness |
| **White** | `o` | **Surprise** | Shock, amazement, startle | Curiosity, mild wonder, intrigue |
| **Brown** | `ae` | **Disgust** | Revulsion, nausea, horror | Distaste, mild aversion, cringe |

## III. Coverage & Boundaries

### A. Within the 7 — Direct Expression

The 7 emotions cover the most frequent emotional expression. Any can be intensified (high-arousal) or softened (low-arousal) through context, lexical choice, and prosody.

### B. Outside the 7 — Lexical Description

Blended emotional states outside the 7 (love, jealousy, awe, shame, guilt, etc.) are expressed through **lexical roots**, not particles. The speaker picks **one** emotional particle as the dominant lens; the full emotional meaning comes from sentence content. The particle acts as an emoji — it tints, it does not define.

> `e, ki ae-rusomena sopa.` — "(With joy,) I look at the room" — warmth foregrounded
> `i, ki ae-rusomena sopa.` — "(With sadness,) I look at the room" — loss foregrounded

See `guide/emotional-register-usage.md` for a full table of blended emotions.

### C. Outside the System Entirely

States requiring self-evaluation, social comparison, or theory of mind (pride, embarrassment, envy, boredom, indignation, schadenfreude) are expressed through dedicated lexical roots and constructions — not particles. The particle system provides assistive register, not a replacement for emotional vocabulary.

## IV. Syntax

Emotional particles are closed-class, toneless, 1–2 syllable function words. See `0-foundation/grammar-syntax.md` §IV-C for the closed-class inventory and exemption rules.

### A. Sentence-Level

The particle **stands alone at the clause head**, before any temporal words:

```
[Emotional Particle]  [Temporal]  [Subject] ... [Verb]
```

> `a, piroi ki kau.` — "(With anger,) I came yesterday."
> `i, paroi si kau nar.` — "(With sadness,) she will not come tomorrow."

### B. Verb-Level

The particle is placed **immediately before the verb** (in the manner-adverb slot):

```
Subject  [Object]  [Obliques]  [Emotional Particle]  [Verb]
```

> `ki bauni a taka.` — "I eat the bread (with anger)."
> `si a-lumi i sopa.` — "She watches the light (with sadness)."

### C. Scope Interaction

- **Sentence-level** sets the emotional frame for the entire clause.
- **Verb-level** overrides the sentence-level register for that specific verb only.

> `i, ki bauni a taka.` — "(Sadness overall,) I eat the bread (with anger)."

Using the **same particle** at both levels intensifies the register:
> `a, ki a taka.` — "(With anger,) I eat (with anger)."

### D. One Particle Per Position

Each position accepts exactly one particle. The speaker chooses the most salient emotional lens.

### E. Word Order — Co-occurrence with Manner Adverbs

Manner adverbs (`-s` derived) describe **how** the action is performed; emotional particles describe the **emotional register**. They co-occur:
> `ki shuks a taka.` — "I quickly eat (with anger)."

The manner adverb precedes the emotional particle. See `0-foundation/grammar-syntax.md` §I-E for the full clause template.

### F. Clause-Initial Fusion — Casual Shorthand

When a clause-initial emotional particle immediately precedes the subject noun, the particle and the noun's colour prefix may **fuse** in casual speech. The comma is dropped; the emotional particle's colour takes orthographic priority:

| Full Form | Fused Form | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `a, a-maeha taka.` | `a-maeha taka.` | "(With anger,) the person eats." |
| `e, a-maeha taka.` | `e-maeha taka.` | "(With joy,) the person eats." |
| `i, y-gilan sopa.` | `i-gilan sopa.` | "(With sadness,) s/he looks at the mountain." |

**Constraints:**
- Only clause-initially, with no intervening words.
- The noun remains **definite** — only the ontological classification is suppressed.
- Fusion is **optional** — the full form is always grammatical and preferred in formal registers.

Fusion creates acknowledged ambiguity (is `a-maeha` fused anger + person, or standard Red ontological prefix?). Context resolves most cases. When the emotional colour matches the noun's 共識 prefix, only the full form unambiguously signals emotional register.

## V. Role in Communication

Emotional particles are an **assistive, optional layer** — comparable to emoji.

- A sentence without a particle is fully grammatical and emotionally neutral.
- Emotional meaning is primarily carried by **lexical roots**; the particle adds an expressive tint.
- Beginners can speak grammatically without touching this system.

## VI. Phonology

### A. Forms

| Colour | Particle | Type |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Red | `a` | Single vowel `/a/` |
| Yellow | `e` | Single vowel `/e/` |
| Blue | `i` | Single vowel `/i/` |
| White | `o` | Single vowel `/o/` |
| Green | `u` | Single vowel `/u/` |
| Black | `y` | Single vowel `/y/` |
| Brown | `ae` | Diphthong `/æ/` |

### B. Tonal Status

All emotional particles are **toneless** (flat mid-tone). See `0-foundation/grammar-syntax.md` §IV-C for the full closed-class inventory and exemption rules.

### C. Connected Speech

Particles are pronounced attached to the following word but orthographically separated by a space. The vowel hiatus between particle and a vowel-initial word (e.g., `a auvltake`) is phonotactically legal; an optional glottal catch may be inserted in careful speech.

## VII. Relationship to Noun Colour Prefixes

The emotional particles and noun colour prefixes share the same 7 forms but serve different grammatical roles:

| | Noun Colour Prefix | Emotional Particle |
|:---|:---|:---|
| **Form** | `a-`, `e-`, `i-`, `o-`, `u-`, `y-`, `ae-` (hyphenated) | `a`, `e`, `i`, `o`, `u`, `y`, `ae` (standalone) |
| **Attaches to** | A noun root | A clause or verb |
| **Encodes** | Ontological class + definiteness | Emotional register |
| **Optional?** | Required for definite nouns | Always optional |

### Simultaneous Use

Both systems operate independently on different grammatical targets:

> `a, a-maeha a taka.`
> — "(With anger,) the-person eats (with anger)."
> - Clause-initial `a`: emotional register = Anger
> - Noun prefix `a-`: ontological class = Red/Alive + definiteness
> - Pre-verbal `a`: verb emotional register = Anger

## VIII. Emotional Colour on Nouns (via Prefix)

> **This section is a summary.** The authoritative specification for emotional override on nouns lives in `1-nominals/nouns-colour-prefix.md` §VI (Speaker Override / 異體字).

A speaker may override a definite noun's 共識 default prefix with a different colour for emotional effect (異體字):

> 共識: `a-fora` (Red) — fire as alive/energy
> 異體字: `y-fora` (Black) — fire tinted with fear
> 異體字: `ae-fora` (Brown) — fire tinted with disgust

On indefinite nouns, a colour prefix may be retained optionally for emotional colouring (`i-lira` — "sadness-tinged water"). See `1-nominals/nouns-colour-prefix.md` §VI for the full specification.
