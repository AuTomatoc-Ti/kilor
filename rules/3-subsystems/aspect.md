## I. Core Philosophy

Kilor has **no grammatical tense** — time is expressed through temporal words and context (see `3-subsystems/temporals.md`). Aspect is handled by an **optional** set of three post-verbal particles. These provide temporal precision when context alone is insufficient.

All aspect particles are **toneless, 1-syllable closed-class function words** exempt from the `-s` constraint (see `0-foundation/grammar-syntax.md` §IV-C).

**Aspect particles are entirely optional.** A bare verb with no aspect particle is grammatical in all contexts and defaults to a habitual or simple reading determined by context and temporal words.

## II. Aspect Particle Inventory

| Particle | Meaning | Position | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| `gin` | progressive — "be X-ing" | Post-verbal | `ki bau taka gin.` — "I'm eating bread." |
| `ger` | perfective — "have X-ed" | Post-verbal | `ki bau taka ger.` — "I've eaten bread." |
| `gou` | experiential — "have X-ed before" | Post-verbal | `ki bau taka gou.` — "I've eaten bread before." |
| (bare verb) | habitual / simple — "X / X-es" | — | `ki bau taka.` — "I eat bread." |

## III. Aspect Details

### A. Progressive — `gin`

`gin` marks an action as **ongoing at the reference time**. It follows the verb:

> `ki bau taka gin.` — "I'm eating bread (right now)."
> `piroi ki kau gin.` — "Yesterday I was coming." (progressive in past time frame)
> `si thep gin.` — "He/she is sleeping."

`gin` is comparable to Cantonese 緊 (gan2) or English "-ing."

### B. Perfective — `ger`

`ger` marks an action as **completed**, with the resulting state relevant at the reference time. It follows the verb:

> `ki bau taka ger.` — "I've eaten bread." (completed, I'm full now)
> `si kau ger.` — "He/she has arrived." (and is here now)
> `ki lira taki ger.` — "I've drunk water." (and am no longer thirsty)

`ger` is comparable to Cantonese 咗 (zo2) or English "have X-ed."

### C. Experiential — `gou`

`gou` marks an action as **experienced at least once before** — the speaker has the life experience of doing X. It follows the verb:

> `ki bau taka gou.` — "I've eaten bread before." (at some point in my life)
> `si te selo kau gou.` — "He/she has been to the market before."
> `ki yre winar gou.` — "I've seen the moon before."

`gou` is comparable to Cantonese 過 (gwo3) or English "have X-ed before."

### D. Contrast: `ger` vs `gou`

| Particle | Focus | Implication |
|---|---|---|
| `ger` | The action is completed; result state is relevant now | "I've eaten (so I'm not hungry)" |
| `gou` | The action happened at least once in experience | "I've eaten that before (it's not new to me)" |

## IV. Interaction with Other Systems

### A. Negation

`nar` scopes over the verb + aspect particle as a unit. Since `nar` follows what it negates, it appears after the aspect particle:

> `ki bau taka gin nar.` — "I'm not eating bread."
> `ki kau ger nar.` — "I haven't arrived yet."
> `ki te selo kau gou nar.` — "I've never been to the market."

### B. Temporal Words

Aspect particles combine freely with temporal words in the clause-initial slot:

> `piroi ki bau taka ger.` — "Yesterday I had already eaten bread."
> `paroi ki bau taka gin.` — "Tomorrow I'll be eating bread." (progressive in future frame)

### C. Imperatives

Aspect particles may appear in imperatives (see `3-subsystems/imperatives.md` §VI-B):

> `Taka ger!` — "Eat!" (urging completion — "Eat up!")

---

## V. Optionality

**Using no aspect particle is always grammatical.** The bare verb covers all temporal readings:

> `ki bau taka.` — "I eat bread." / "I'm eating bread." / "I ate bread." / "I've eaten bread."
> *(Disambiguated by temporal word or context)*

Aspect particles are precision tools — they clarify when context is insufficient. They are not required for grammaticality.
