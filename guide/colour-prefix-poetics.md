# Kilor Colour-Prefix Poetics — Artistic Colour Resurrection

**Module:** Rhetorical device for the ontological colour prefix
**Last updated:** 2026-08-07
**Spec reference:** `rules/1-nominals/nouns-colour-prefix.md` §I-A, §IV

---

## I. What This Is

In ordinary grammar the colour prefix is **not** a colour word — it encodes ontology + definiteness (hyphen) or an emotional/proper override (apostrophe), and literal hue lives in dedicated colour words (renewal, e.g. `awin` = "red"). See `rules/1-nominals/nouns-colour-prefix.md` §I-A and §IV.

But Kilor's prefixes *descend from* colour terms, and poets may **deliberately resurrect that covert origin** — reading an ontological prefix as if it still carried its hue. This is the **fossil-colour** register: a marked, artistic double-voicing, **not** productive grammar.

---

## II. The Double-Voicing Device

A poet writes `a-fora`. Ordinary grammar reads:

> `a-fora` — "the fire" (definite, framed as living/energy)

The poetic reading summons both layers at once:

> `a-fora` — "the **red**, living fire"

The hue arrives as an *echo* — an archaism deliberately staged by the poet, so the reader feels the etymological past alongside the modern meaning. Compare English poetry resurrecting the archaic sense of *quick* ("the quick and the dead" = *living*).

---

## III. The Renewal Tension — `a-` vs `awin`

The sharpest expressive resource is the gap between the **fossil** prefix and the **live** colour word:

> `a-fora` vs `awin fora`

- **`a-fora`** = the fire, with a colour flicker from the past — subjective, auratic.
- **`awin fora`** = literal "red fire" — objective, flat, descriptive.

A poet can play them against each other, and even refuse the modern word to heighten the fossil:

> `a-fora, not awin fora` — "the fire — not merely a *red* fire"

The speaker foregrounds ontology (living energy) while consciously *withholding* the modern colour word, letting the ancient layer still shimmer.

---

## IV. Relation to Emotional Register

Fossil colour is a **different device** from the 異體字 emotional override:

- **異體字** (apostrophe, `y'fora`) changes the *framing/emotion* — "the fire [in fear]".
- **Fossil colour** (hyphen, archaic reading) changes the *reading back to hue* — "the red fire".

They are not the same, and they can combine: `y'fora` read with a fossil sense summons "the black, dread fire" — hue and fear layered together. See `guide/emotional-register-usage.md` for the emotional side.

---

## V. Guidelines

- **Marked, deliberate, rare.** Resurrection is a choice; overuse cheapens it. Reserve it for moments that want mythic or archaic weight.
- **Root meaning stays dominant.** As everywhere, the root carries the semantic weight; the fossil colour tints without redefining.
- **Not a literal reading.** Outside poetry, `a-fora` is simply "the fire (living)". The colour reading exists only where the poet stages it.
- **This is poetry, not grammar.** The techniques above are aesthetic conventions, not prescriptive rules.
